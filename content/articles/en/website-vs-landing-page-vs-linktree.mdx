## The Question People Ask First, Even Though It's Not the One That Matters

A lot of business owners start with the wrong question: "do I even need a website?" The better question is: **what do I want people to do after they find my business online?**

Linktree, landing pages, and websites are often treated as three versions of the same thing — just different looks. In reality, each does a completely different job. Pick the wrong one, and you'll feel like you "have an online presence" when what you actually have is an empty storefront.

## Linktree: A Bunch of Doors, Not a House

Linktree (and similar tools) is essentially a list of buttons. It has one job: routing traffic from your Instagram/TikTok bio somewhere else — a marketplace catalog, a WhatsApp number, or a specific post.

**Makes sense when:**
- You're just starting out and every transaction still happens in DMs/WhatsApp
- You sell across multiple platforms at once (Shopee, Tokopedia, Instagram) and just need one entry point
- Your budget is genuinely zero

**Its limit:** Linktree can't tell a story. There's no room to explain what makes your business different, nowhere to put testimonials, product photos in context, or reasons people should trust you. The moment someone clicks a button, they leave Linktree — you lose them from your business's "home page."

## Landing Page: One Page, One Goal

A landing page is a single page built for one specific action — usually a campaign, a particular product, or a time-limited promo. Every element on that page — headline, image, testimonials, button — points toward one decision: click, chat, or buy.

**Makes sense when:**
- You're running ads (Meta Ads/Google Ads) for one specific product/promo
- You want to A/B test marketing messages without touching your entire website
- The focus is narrow and temporary — not a representation of your whole business

**Its limit:** A landing page isn't built to be browsed. Someone curious about your other services, wanting to know more about your company, or looking to check your portfolio won't find those answers there. That's not a flaw — it's simply not what it's for.

## Website: Your Business's Permanent Home

A website is where everything about your business lives in one system — profile, services/products, portfolio, contact, and (if needed) operational systems like booking or a dynamic catalog. It's what shows up when someone googles your business name, the reference point when a prospective client wants to "check first before trusting you," and something you control completely — unlike social media, whose rules can change at any time.

**Makes sense when:**
- Your business is already running and needs to look credible to new prospective clients
- You're tired of explaining the same things over and over in DMs (pricing, services, how it works)
- You want to show up in Google search, not just rely on social media algorithms

**The entry point doesn't have to be expensive.** A clean one-page website — business profile, services, and a direct WhatsApp button — is already enough to move past the "just a Linktree" phase. From there, a website can grow step by step: more pages, booking features, all the way to connected automation systems.

## Quick Comparison Table

| | Linktree | Landing Page | Website |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Main function** | List of links | Single-action conversion | Full digital presence |
| **Lifespan** | Always active, content keeps changing | Temporary (per campaign) | Permanent, grows with the business |
| **Discoverable on Google?** | No | Limited | Yes (with baseline SEO) |
| **Best for business stage** | Just starting out | Any business running a campaign | Already running / ready to level up |
